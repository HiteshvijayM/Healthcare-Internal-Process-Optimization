## 7. Assumptions — flag for review

Recorded under CHG-019. These are the choices made while authoring `specs/001-admin-workflow-assistant/spec.md` where the source feature request did not state a value. The governing rule applied was: **adopt the project's already-approved value where one exists; invent nothing that would silently reopen a change-logged decision.**

**Adopted from approved policy (feature.md §5.4 and §7) — not newly invented:**

- Provisional routing requires routing confidence ≥ 0.80 with `patient_reference` and `requested_service` both present, and is never permitted while a critical signal is active or a clearance gate is pending (P1, safety-bearing).
- "Large majority" was read as ≥ 85% field extraction accuracy and ≥ 90% first-pass completeness; routing correctness ≥ 9 of 10 graded cases; seeded omissions detected 100%; unapproved sends exactly 0 (§7).
- "A short time" for first action was read as under 30 seconds from intake to draft ready (§7).
- Approval SLAs are 2 business days routine, 4 hours urgent, 30 minutes critical acknowledgement, with early warning at 80% of SLA elapsed and breach recorded at 100% (P4, P5).
- Duplicate detection uses a 72-hour window on a sender + patient reference + requested service key, independent of arrival channel (P2).
- Escalation packets require 100% of the seven mandatory fields with no partial sends (P3, safety-bearing).
- Rework loops are capped at 2 before mandatory human escalation (P6).
- Repeatability tolerance is ±2 aggregate percentage points with 100% identical per-case classifications (P7).
- Audit retention is project lifetime, minimum 90 days (P8).
- The interaction surface is a single conversational web/in-app surface, one demo tenant, one authenticated reviewer session (P9).
- The routing queue set is fixed at Insurance, Operations, Diagnostics, Legal, Finance, with fixed declarative rules (§13.5, CHG-013).
- Sample data is SYN-CASESET-v1; no external dataset, no real patient data at any point (§13.2, CHG-013, constitution §3).
- Roles are authorities rather than individuals, taken from harness §4.1; the assistant holds no role in that registry (CHG-016).
- The manual comparison baseline follows the §13.3 protocol (CHG-017).

**Chosen conservatively by this specification where policy was silent:**

- On duplicate match the item is flagged and held for human adjudication — never auto-discarded, never auto-merged. P2 defines the window and key but not the outcome; holding is the option that cannot lose a legitimate resend.
- An incomplete escalation packet is held and a blocker is raised to both the designated Clinical Authority and the Intake Coordinator, with the critical signal kept visibly active until the packet is complete and routed.
- Escalation-packet dispatch requires recorded approval by a designated Escalation Dispatch Approver, who must not be the packet's own clinical recipient; where none is designated the packet is held under a governance blocker rather than dispatched. The source request described escalation as automatic; the non-negotiable constraint that every outbound action carries a recorded human approval takes precedence, so automation was limited to packet preparation plus a non-suppressible approval prompt. The §4.1 registry names no role authorized to approve dispatch, so the spec requires the designation to exist rather than assigning one. **Recommended resolution (§8 item 9): Intake Coordinator, with Team Lead as named alternate**, since dispatch approval is administrative rather than clinical and separation of duty from the clinical recipient then holds automatically. This requires a §4.1 registry amendment before it becomes binding.
- The dispatch-approval deadline is deliberately left unbound in the spec. `feature.md` §5.4 requires every scored numeric threshold to live in the approved policy table, and no dispatch-approval deadline exists there. FR-052 therefore requires only that an approved deadline exists and is strictly shorter than the approved 30-minute critical acknowledgement window (P4), and raises a governance blocker where none has been approved. A candidate value of 15 minutes was originally proposed but is **not** treated as normative. **Recommended resolution (§8 item 10): 10 minutes**, so that the dispatch check — a completeness check, not a judgement call — consumes only a third of the acknowledgement window rather than half of it. This requires a policy-table amendment before it becomes binding.
- Escalation packets are prepared within the same 30-second draft-readiness bound used for ordinary drafts (§7), rather than under a separate unstated timing rule.
- Clearance ordering was initially read as clinical first, then financial, from the acceptance scenario wording; on review it was **relaxed to order-independent** (FR-033), since the safety property is the conjunction of both gates rather than their sequence. AS-11 still passes unchanged because order-independence is a superset of the source scenario.
- Completion-task ownership resolves through an inspectable field-to-role mapping over the §4.1 registry, defaulting to the Intake Coordinator where a field has no mapped owner.
- Rejected work returns to the stage that produced it and never past the completeness check gate.

### What still blocks planning

Five ratifications are outstanding. None is an open question — each is an amendment to an artifact that already exists, and in every case the specification behaves safely and unchanged until the amendment lands:

1. **Item 9** — the §4.1 approver registry must gain the Escalation Dispatch Approver authority. Until it does, FR-051 and FR-054 correctly hold every escalation packet under a governance blocker.
2. **Item 10** — the §5.4 policy table must gain a dispatch-approval deadline. Until it does, FR-052 correctly blocks.
3. **Item 3** — P4 currently defines global SLA targets only, so the per-service-line override model needs a policy-table amendment. Until then every item resolves to the approved global default.
4. **Item 5** — P2 defines the windowed key match only, so the unbounded document-identity match extends approved duplicate policy.
5. **Item 2** — `feature.md` §7 states an unqualified `< 30 seconds`; grading it at the 95th percentile is a relaxation of an approved metric.

**Item 8's contents** remain a clinical deliverable. The specification is complete and internally consistent without them, but no escalation behaviour can be meaningfully exercised until the register is populated.

### Review round 6 findings and resolution

An independent consistency review of the applied changes returned six blocking findings, all of which were repaired before this entry was recorded. Two are worth naming because they were genuine defects introduced by the resolution round rather than pre-existing gaps:

- **The acknowledgement clock's start event was undefined.** FR-056 had been scoped to check on-call coverage *downstream* of dispatch, while FR-052 simultaneously described the dispatch deadline as consuming part of an already-running acknowledgement window. Those two models cannot both hold. The clock is now explicitly defined to start **at detection**, so it measures elapsed clinical risk rather than elapsed administrative handling — which is the only reading under which a sub-window dispatch deadline is meaningful. Consequently the coverage check became a fourth governance designation inside FR-054's precedence rule rather than a separate post-dispatch condition, and FR-024, SC-011 and the Q5 clarification were aligned to four designations.
- **FR-057 raised a blocker without stopping progression.** Because no signal has been detected when the register fails to resolve, FR-011's prohibition on provisional routing did not engage, so FR-010 could still have advanced the case. FR-057 now explicitly holds the case and refuses provisional routing until a valid register version resolves.

The remaining four findings were repaired by making downstream SLA references point at the applied value (FR-052, FR-056, SC-010), defining document identity and its normalization rules in FR-055, specifying the percentile estimator and graded population in SC-003, and adding acceptance coverage so that FR-055, FR-056 and FR-057 are each exercised in both directions — including a register entry that must *not* match, an absent register, absent coverage, and multiple simultaneously absent designations.
